System & Phase

  • System: a bounded space or exact quantity of a material substance.
  • Dispersion: ≥2 phases — a dispersed (internal) phase inside a continuous (external) phase.
  • Phase: a distinct homogeneous part of a system, separated by definite boundaries.

Dispersion by particle size

  • True solution — molecular level, invisible by microscopy.
  • Colloidal dispersion — 1–500 nm (e.g. blood).
  • Coarse dispersion — >500 nm / 0.5 μm (emulsions, suspensions).

Colligative properties

  • Depend on the ratio of total solute particles to total solvent particles — not on solute identity.
  • Equal concentrations of different non‑electrolytes ⇒ approximately equal colligative effect.

Additive vs constitutive

  • Additive: sum of contributions of atoms/constituents — e.g. molecular weight.
  • Constitutive: depends on arrangement & kind of atoms — e.g. electric properties.

Non‑electrolytes vs electrolytes

  • Non‑electrolytes: don't ionize, don't conduct current. Regular colligative effect. E.g. sucrose, glycerin, naphthalene.
  • Electrolytes: ionize, conduct current, apparent (larger) colligative effect. E.g. HCl, NaCl.

Strong vs weak electrolytes

  • Strong: completely ionized — HCl, sodium sulfate.
  • Weak: only partly ionized — ephedrine, phenobarbital.

Molarity / Normality

Mole = wt / M.wt


M = mole / liter of solution


N = gram‑equivalents / liter

  • Both change with temperature (liquid expansion/contraction).

Molality & mole fraction

m = moles solute / kg solvent


X₁ = n₁ /(n₁+n₂)

  • Weight‑based ⇒ independent of temperature.

Percentage expressions

  • % w/w: g solute / 100 g solution
  • % v/v: mL solute / 100 mL solution
  • % w/v: g solute / 100 mL solution
  • mg %: mg solute / 100 mL solution
